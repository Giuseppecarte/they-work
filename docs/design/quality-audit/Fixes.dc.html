01
The published image has no locale
The renderer gates quadrants on a UTF-8 locale. The runtime container sets none, so every user lands on half-blocks — the worst rung — regardless of their terminal. One line in the Dockerfile moves everyone up two rungs.
docker/Dockerfile
ONE LINE
02
Sextants are gated on a variable containers never get
The check reads TERM_PROGRAM, which Docker does not forward and the documented command does not pass. In a container it is always absent, so the best character rung can never be selected by the people who actually run this.
theywork-render
canvas.rs
SMALL
03
The sign renders the wrong letters
THEY-WORK draws as THEY-HOAI! — W becomes H, R becomes A, K becomes an exclamation. The letterform table is correct, so diagonals are being lost when glyph pixels are drawn. It is the largest text in the product and the first thing anyone reads.
theywork-render
views/office.rs
MEDIUM
04
Nothing verifies what we publish
Every check runs against a local build, where the locale and terminal checks happen to pass. Both defects above existed only in the artefact. A gate that pulls the published tag, runs it through a pty and asserts on what it draws would have caught them the day they shipped.

05
The extra pixels are not buying detail
At the graphics rung a worker is ten times larger but no richer: no glasses, beanie, headphones or desk objects, and the five look alike. The wardrobe's whole premise is recognising someone before you read their name plate. Walls also render near-black where the design has them violet.

REAL WORK
06
Nobody has watched a graphics frame on a terminal
Kitty, Sixel and iTerm2 are covered only by byte-level tests. The frame in this dashboard was captured from the renderer, not from a window. Until someone runs it in Windows Terminal or on a Mac, the top rung is theory.

MINUTES
THE FIRST TWO ARE NEARLY FREE
A locale line and a capability check. Together they take the shipped picture from 160×96 to 320×144 without touching a single sprite — and on a Sixel-capable terminal, straight to the top rung. That is most of the visible gap, closed by packaging.
THE LAST ONE IS THE ACTUAL DESIGN WORK
Everything above is a defect. Number five is not — it is the gap between having the pixels and spending them. That is where the design boards in docs/design/ stop being aspirational, and it is the only item here that needs judgement rather than a fix.
